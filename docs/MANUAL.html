CYBERSUNDAYHow to play

A first-person cyberpunk RPG about debt. You start with four hit points, no abilities, no money, and rent due at dawn. This manual covers every control and the mechanics that are not obvious from playing.

Controller

Left stick moves, right stick looks. A jump · B crouch · X reload · Y interact · RT fire · LT aim · L1/R1 lean · L3 flashlight · R3 grapple · D-pad hotbar 1–4.

Everything else — takedown, adopt, wait, throw, hold-weapon, minimap zoom, waypoint, all six tabs, quicksave, night vision, slots 5–10 and wheel cycling — has no pad default. Leaning on a pad is unconditional, since L1/R1 aren't shared. The pad goes silent when the window loses focus.

Avoid the End key

End instantly restarts the current level and loses unsaved progress. It is a leftover developer key and it is live in this build. Home prints a harmless technical readout. F1–F4 and ` are developer tools and do nothing in a normal build.

02 First Five Minutes

First launch plays two black warning cards you cannot skip, then a Terms of Service screen you have to accept — declining only offers Quit. On later launches any key skips the cards.

Character creation is zero-sum. Every stat starts at 0 and is clamped to −5…+10. You raise one only by lowering another; you can never come out net-positive, though you may deliberately underspend. Begin stays greyed out until you type a character name — if the button looks broken, it's the name field, not your stats.

Then the Ledger. The second screen rates your build, hands you a credit score and spending limit, and offers the whole implant catalogue as a shopping cart. There is no free starting implant — everything here is bought on credit and lands on your ledger as debt. Buying nothing is fine and never blocks Begin. A dumped stat sheet lowers your credit limit, so bad stats cost you implants too.

You have 4 hit points

That is not a typo. A pistol round does 1. You also start with nothing but your fists, zero zorkmids, and your weapon holstered.

Practically, in order

  1. Learn the draw. Your weapon starts stowed — the first left click only pulls it out.
  2. Find something to fight. Kills pay 25 XP and 1–4 zorkmids.
  3. Press F on everything that looks like a person or a container.
  4. Watch the clock. Rent is 20 zorkmids a day and the first notice lands at your first dawn — roughly seven and a half minutes into a fresh run.

03 Movement & Stamina

Base speed is 5.0 m/s, but walking is the default — running is opt-in on Shift, and without it you move at 0.7× (3.5 m/s). Aiming stacks a further 0.4× on top of the walk tier, so aimed movement is about 28% of full speed. Crouching is 0.5× and skips the walk multiplier. In the air you can always change direction — the faster you are travelling the wider the turn, so a running jump commits you and a light hop does not. What you cannot do is manufacture much speed: from a standstill you build to about 1.75 m/s and carry roughly 1.3 metres. The run-up is what buys distance. Jumping fires you off at 15% more than the speed you carried into it, so a full sprint leaves the ground at 5.75 m/s and clears about 4.6 metres — three and a half times a standing hop, and further still off a bunny-hop chain. Letting go of the keys mid-air no longer slows you down, and nothing you launched with is taken away: a chain, a grapple fling and an air dash keep every metre per second they gave you — you can just aim them now.

Most of the movement kit is bought, not given

A fresh character has zero abilities. Slide, wall climb, bunny hop, air dash, fall immunity and the grapple are each a separate microchip you must find or buy, then pay a mechanic to install. Without the chip, the key does nothing. Pressing Space at a wall on a fresh save and getting nothing is correct behaviour, not a bug.

Empty the bar by sprinting and you lose sprint for three full seconds no matter how fast it refills. Jumping is not locked that long — it fails only while the pool is actually at zero and returns about a third of a second later. At zero, jump, slide, dash, grapple and fists all refuse. Guns are the deliberate exception: they keep firing.

Falling

  • Below 16 m/s of impact speed: free.
  • Above that: 0.5 HP per m/s over the threshold at the 4 max HP you start with — the cost scales with your max HP, so a drop always takes the same slice of your bar and a Strength build pays proportionally more per m/s rather than growing out of the danger.
  • With 4 HP a 24 m/s landing kills you instantly; at 1 HP that drops to 18 m/s.
  • Falling for four unbroken seconds kills you regardless of impact, even with Fall Immunity.
  • Crouching on landing does not reduce fall damage. It only softens the camera.
The grey screen is a warning, not a bug

The screen drains to grey as you fall. Full grey means the next contact with the ground kills you at your current HP. A survivable hop never tints at all. The grey also ramps during the last two seconds before the four-second void death.

Leaning and stairs

Lean slides your camera 0.45 m sideways and rolls it 12°. Your hitbox does not move — you can see and shoot around cover without exposing your body, but enemies still shoot where your body actually is. Holding both sides puts you upright. Lean refuses during dialogue, with a menu open, or while dead.

You automatically step up ledges and risers to 0.6 m while walking — no jump needed — and the camera glides over the step rather than snapping.

Bunny-hopping

With the Bunny Hop implant installed and switched on: land, then jump again within 0.4 seconds while holding a movement direction. Each clean hop adds 1.2 m/s to the speed you carried into it — the chain compounds, so it genuinely accelerates you rather than snapping you to a fixed tier. That means your run-up still matters with the chip on: entering a chain at a sprint reaches the 12 m/s ceiling in about eight hops, while starting from a walk takes longer to wind up. This is the difference the implant buys — without it a jump gets only the flat 15% take-off boost, which cannot stack and decays straight back to running speed.

  • A standing jump never chains — it earns nothing.
  • Staying on the ground past the window kills the chain.
  • Crouching kills the chain, and so does being over-encumbered. The jump still fires, it just earns nothing and wipes what you had.
  • Switching the implant off drops any banked chain immediately.

The look-sensitivity falloff above 6.5 m/s exists so hop-runs stay controllable. It is not a bug and it is not implant-gated.

04 Combat

Full-auto is the default — pistol, SMG, shotgun and fists fire while held. Knife, sniper and grenade launcher are one click per shot. Aiming divides spread by three, extends instant-hit reach by half, and locks you to a crawl. Fists cannot aim, which is why right-click is free to be your second fist.

Shooting costs stamina — and freezes its recovery

Every shot spends stamina: SMG ~1.2, pistol ~1.8, sniper ~2.3, shotgun ~7.2, grenade launcher ~14.4 out of a pool of 100. Worse, you never regenerate between your own shots — one trigger pull locks recovery for at least 1.5 seconds, and for the whole reload if that shot emptied the magazine. The sniper locks it for 3.5 seconds every single shot. Movement only locks recovery for 0.35 s; firing is in a different league.

Reloading throws away the rounds in your magazine

Ammo is counted in whole magazines, not loose bullets. Reloading a half-empty gun ejects that partial mag — those rounds are gone — and seats a fresh full one. Reload when empty, not when convenient. You start with 4 magazines per calibre. You can swap away mid-reload; the stowed gun keeps loading at two-thirds speed.

Accuracy is a stance choice

Your shots do not land exactly on the crosshair, and the crosshair does not show you where they go. The reticle stays pinned dead centre; the drift is something you feel in your results. Aim wander runs from 2.0° standing to 5.0° at full speed. Stand completely still for 1.4 seconds and it tightens to a sixth of that — then move and you lose all of it in 0.35 seconds. Crouching multiplies by 0.4, aiming by 0.35, and Gunplay makes it 8% steadier per point.

There is no muzzle climb. No shipped gun kicks your aim upward. What you get instead is per-weapon screen shake — the SMG barely nudges, the sniper and launcher saturate it in one shot.

Inside effective range a shot hits instantly. Past it the shot becomes a real flying bullet that still hurts but takes time to arrive and can be dodged. Headshot and sneak multipliers stack — a sneak headshot with the knife is 24 damage against enemies who mostly have single-digit health, making it the best stealth weapon in the game. Enemies cannot headshot you at all.

Overkill punches through: excess damage carries on and hits whoever is behind. Damage is also tracked per limb — crippled legs halve movement, a crippled arm widens that shooter's spread. This works on you too, and only a Healer mends limbs.

Reading enemies

The coloured outline is their attitude. Red hostile, green friendly, blue companion, black neutral, deepening as they close. The instant a hostile locks onto you its ring goes full red at any distance, right across the map, fading when you break the lock. Full red means that one is hunting you now.

Enemies telegraph every shot. An armed enemy paints a laser at you that brightens as its shot charges — fully lit means it is about to fire. Half a second before impact you hear a warning beep. Red arcs grow around your crosshair pointing at each enemy drawing a bead, sized by incoming damage.

Enemies never hitscan, and they lead you. Every enemy shot is a real travelling bullet at half your bullet speed — but they aim where you will be, so a steady held strafe will not save you. Change direction, use cover, or close the distance.

Fences and windows are not cover

Anything drawn as mesh, wire, glass or foliage stops bullets from neither side. Enemies see you through a fence and shoot you through it — and so can you. It still blocks walking, thrown objects, the grapple and the interact ray. Find solid cover.

Three things worth knowing

Holster to talk your way out — once

If you attacked a neutral or friendly and they turned on you, hold R to holster and they stand down: the grudge drops and the reputation you lost is restored. It works once per person. Genuinely hostile factions were never provoked and are unaffected.

Bullet time

Start aiming while airborne and the world drops to 40% speed for 0.7 seconds. Your first shot ends it instantly, as do landing and releasing aim. One per jump — and a scope entered on the ground then launched does not arm it.

You cannot draw a gun with your hands full

The moment you pick up a physics prop your weapon is stowed and locked. Clicking won't draw it, hold-R won't, switching weapons won't. Put the prop down first. Left click while carrying throws the prop instead.

The Gunsmith Bench

Six slots per gun — Receiver, Barrel, Magazine, Sight, Muzzle, Stock. Fitting costs 40% of the part's value; removing costs only 20% and you keep the part, so experimenting is safe. Every refusal is checked before you are charged. Mods trade one stat against another — there is no pure upgrade. A Suppressor cuts your gunshot's carry to a third and kills the muzzle flash at 8% less damage; a Long Barrel adds range and tightens spread but slows you; a Match Receiver adds 30% damage and fires 10% faster, paying for it with 15% more stamina per shot, a fitting bill 1.3× the usual, and a Gunplay of 6 before the bench will fit it at all.

Health and death

Health regenerates on its own — but only six quiet seconds after the fight really ends. Quiet means you haven't fired, haven't taken damage, and no hostile holds you as its target. A full refill takes about 83 seconds. The tell is audible: your low-HP heartbeat softens the moment healing starts. Sitting in a conversation does not heal you.

Death is a respawn, not a reload. You get a death card, then revive at your last checkpoint with full HP, full stamina and healed limbs — and the world stays exactly as you left it. Your new life starts holstered. The cinematic is click-to-skip: half a second in, a click (attack, Enter or plain left mouse) runs it eight times over and lands you on the death card, and once that card has been readable for another half second a second click returns you to play. Nothing is cut — every beat still fires in order, so you can hurry past your own death but not past being told who killed you.

Your wallet dies with you

Whoever killed you pockets your cash — hunt them down and loot it back. If nothing killed you it spills on the ground as a physics money bag where you fell. Money in the bank is untouched. Bank anything you can't afford to lose.

05 Interface & Inventory

Menus do not pause the game

Opening the inventory, map, a shop, an ATM or even Settings pauses nothing. The city keeps running and you can be shot while you read. Your character just stops responding to input. The only thing that freezes the world is a conversation — during which you are also immune to damage.

The backpack

A 6-wide × 5-tall Tetris grid — 30 cells. Every item is a rectangle, so what fits is about shape, not count. World containers are roomier at 10 × 8.

A stack that can't fit isn't lost — it appears in an overflow strip under the grid. The line beneath shows carry weight against capacity, reading ENCUMBERED in red when you're over. Encumbrance blocks the bunny-hop chain, wall climb and grapple reel, and lowers your jump.

Money is not an inventory item

Zorkmids are a wallet number, not a tile — they never eat backpack space. The amount sits above the grid and top-left on the HUD, with a floating +N/−N on every change, turning red while negative. To drop cash use the "Drop…" button on the wallet row. Loot money on corpses is a coin tile, which converts when you take it. There is no Sort button in your own bag — Sort exists on the shop screen only.

The HUD

  • Bottom-left — HP bar with the ammo line "clip / reserve" above it.
  • Top-left — zorkmids, plus an "Owed" figure whenever you're in the red.
  • Top-right — minimap, clock below it.
  • Top-centre — heading tape, stealth badge, detection meter, enemy health bar.
  • Centre — crosshair with the stamina arc around it, and the name of what you're looking at.

Every hit pops an X and a confirm ding. The ding's pitch drops as the target nears death, and drops deeper on a headshot — you can hear how close they are without looking.

The map

The corner minimap is north-up by default — the plan stays axis-locked while your caret spins; Accessibility → Rotate Minimap flips it to heading-up — and K cycles three zoom steps. The Map tab is always north-up. On the Map tab: wheel zooms about your cursor, left-drag pans, clicking empty ground places a pin, clicking a pin selects it, and clicking again opens its editor for name, note, mark and colour. X in the world pins whatever you're looking at, auto-names it, and makes it your tracked marker.

You need a scanner implant to see people on the map

Without a scanner chip installed and switched on, the map draws no bodies at all — at any zoom, on both the corner minimap and the Map tab. Bio Scanner shows bodies within 22 m, Deep Scanner within 55 m; own both and the widest enabled one wins. Also check Accessibility → NPCs On Minimap.

Glyph alphabet — shape first, colour second

Bodies are small filled shapes: filled triangle hostile, filled diamond companion, filled circle friendly, hollow ring neutral bystander. A hostile that has noticed you grows a ring that steps outward as its suspicion rises. Stations are larger outlined shapes: shop diamond, bank hexagon, clinic cross, trainer downward triangle (so it can never be mistaken for a hostile), tech square, leisure circle, exit chevron. Markers on another floor fade and grow a tick showing above or below.

The noise ring is your own footprint

The circle pulsing around your caret on the minimap is exactly how far your noise currently carries, drawn to true world scale — the same number the AI tests against. If a body dot sits inside it, that body is close enough to have heard you. Firing bursts it past the edge of the box. It shows your noise only, never sound made at you, and it is a worst case: walls attenuate it, and only hostiles act on it.

Stations, looting and saving

Walk up and press F, or pick the option in an NPC's conversation. None of these pause the world — don't stop at a counter mid-firefight. Shops put the merchant's stock on the left and your bag on the right; the ATM is a five-line statement where your account is one signed number, so "deposit" and "pay off debt" are the same button. A Healer restores full HP and clears all limb damage. The chip installer takes two clicks per row because it spends money and destroys the chip.

Loot with F on a corpse, container, or a live NPC while sneaking. A weapon they're actually holding shows as a padlock and cannot be lifted.

Saving: F5 quicksaves, F9 quickloads, and there are 3 manual slots in Settings → Save / Load. One autosave acts as the run's checkpoint and is what "Continue" resumes.

The autosave is a profile checkpoint, not a world snapshot

It does not restore looted containers, dead NPCs, dropped loot, spawned enemies or NPC positions — and every gun comes back with a full magazine. Only the quicksave and the 3 manual slots carry the exact-world layer. If you want to resume a situation, use F5.

06 Progression & Implants

Two currencies — don't confuse them

Skill points, earned from XP levels, buy perks. Zorkmids buy stats. They are not interchangeable.

XP comes from three places: kills at 25 XP flat, but only for someone you personally damaged — a companion's kill or a death you merely engineered pays zero; quest completion; and the first read of a note or terminal. Silent takedowns do pay. There is no way to lose XP. Level 1 is 100 XP and each level costs 50 more than the last, so four kills is level 1 and ten kills is level 2. Every level hands you one skill point.

All linear, no cap and no diminishing returns until a value physically bottoms out — aim sway reaches perfectly still around Gunplay 13, and the enemy detection meter stops filling entirely around Larceny 20. Agility’s two combat effects bottom out on a safety floor rather than at zero: the knife stops getting faster around Agility 13 and the fists around 15, and reloads stop shortening around Agility 16 (the sniper, with the longest reload in the game, keeps improving to 19).

A stat raise is refused while your account is in the red

Paid stat raises can never be funded on credit. If the trainer won't sell you a point despite visible cash, settle your ledger at an ATM first. This is the single most likely "the game is broken" report.

Implants: installed vs active

Implants come as microchips. Holding a chip does nothing — carry it to a mechanic and pay to have it fitted. Fitting a chip you found costs half its value; buying one from the mechanic costs 1.25× value plus the install fee, so exploring pays roughly 3.5× better than shopping.

Press I. Installed means it's in your head and paid for, permanently. Active is a per-row toggle — switch an implant off and gameplay stops using it, but you still own it, and the off state survives quitting. The tab also lists chips sitting in your backpack that aren't installed yet.

07 The World

Zorkmids and the ledger

Kills pay 1 zorkmid base — 2 if the killing blow was a headshot, 4 if every point of damage you did was a headshot. Past 30 m you get 2 plus 0.1 per extra metre, capped at 8. Collateral and trick shots pay too. NPCs bank their own bounties, so a raider who has been winning fights is a richer corpse.

Cash pays first, then bank savings, then credit — and paying from the bank or on credit adds a 3% service charge, so carrying cash is always cheapest. Your bank account is one signed number: positive is savings, negative is what you owe. You can never withdraw into credit, and every deposit is eaten by debt first. Banked money survives death; pocket cash does not.

Rent is due at dawn

The Ledger charges 20 zorkmids per in-game day. At your first dawn you get a notice serving the terms and one day of grace, then the charge lands every dawn. You start with zero zorkmids and the first dawn arrives about seven and a half minutes into a fresh run. Rent can never push you into debt — it takes what you have down to zero and logs the shortfall against your record. Waiting through several dawns pays several rents. Bank interest posts at dawn too: savings grow 0.5% a day, debt compounds at 2%.

Time

A full in-game day is 600 real seconds. A new game opens at noon; day runs 06:00–20:00. NPC schedules, streetlights and the sky all key off it. T opens the Wait panel (1–24 hours), which heals 2 HP per hour but does not mend limbs — and it is not free, since the hours genuinely pass and rent falls due exactly as if you'd lived them. Waiting is refused while a hostile is aware of you.

Reputation

Every faction scores −100 to +100, starting at 0. Provoking a member costs 30 points; killing one costs 12 — even a hostile one. Saving someone by killing their attacker pays 15. At −25 a faction turns hostile and attacks on sight; at +25 it can turn friendly. Standing also bends merchant prices and can unlock stock a low-rep player never sees. A kill penalty is never reversed; a provoke can be, once, via the holster pardon. Press V for a live bar per faction.

Stealth

A typical enemy has 25 m of sight in a 110° cone, needs line of sight, and takes about a second of continuous sight to lock on. It's a meter, not a switch — a glimpse is not an instant alert, and it drains when they lose you. Snipers are the exception at 150 m and four seconds. Crouching halves the range you can be spotted at and silences your footsteps.

Crouch buys silent walking. A rooftop drop is nearly as loud as a gunshot.

Light matters both ways. Standing in shadow makes the detection meter fill up to four times slower. Carrying a lit flashlight is the opposite trade — you're spotted 60% further out and locked on twice as fast.

A badge sits top-centre: HIDDEN nobody has you · DETECTED a meter is filling · CAUTION someone lost you and is searching · DANGER someone has locked on. You always see the worst state in the level. When someone first notices you, a white "!" pops over their head with a sting, and it reads through walls.

Breaking line of sight is not instant escape. An alerted enemy coasts after your actual position for two more seconds — so when it starts searching it heads where you went, not where it lost you. Then it hunts for four seconds before forgetting you.

Silent takedown: with the chip installed, crouch behind an unaware enemy, get within 1.5 m inside a 120° rear arc, and hold Q for three seconds. The kill credits you fully and the witness shout is suppressed — but the body is still discoverable later. Larceny shortens the hold.

Pets

Aim at a stray dog and tap B to adopt it — you name it and it follows you. Hold B on a claimed pet to release it. Hold Q aimed at a friendly animal to pet it. Companions can be told "Follow me" or "Wait here" in conversation.

08 Settings & Comfort

Nothing applies until you press Apply

Every slider, toggle and cycler is staged. Revert — or just closing and reopening — throws your edits away. Two exceptions apply instantly: key rebinds, and the Audio music-folder picker. Note that dragging a volume slider doesn't move the bus until Apply, so its audible tick auditions the volume you're leaving.

If the retro look makes you ill

This game renders itself deliberately degraded. Under Accessibility, every one of these is genuinely free to turn off — the shader early-outs rather than running an identity pass:

Under Video, Presentation flips between Crisp (real resolution) and Retro (a ~792×444 buffer blown up — the chunky pixel look). Dithering to 0%, Color Depth to "24-bit (Off)", Ink Outline to 0% and Muzzle Smoke to 0% each remove a distinct layer of the effect.

Photosensitivity

Screen Flashes off kills every full-screen flash at once — hurt, dash, kill, sky pop, muzzle. Hit Stop off removes the micro-freeze on impacts. Low-HP Heartbeat off silences just the heartbeat without touching any volume slider.

Everyone speaks out loud

Text-to-Speech is on by default. Every dialogue line and every combat shout is read aloud by an offline synthesiser — a flat robot voice yelling at you across a street is the game working, not a glitch. It rides the Audio tab's Voice slider, so you can turn it down without turning it off; Accessibility → Text-to-Speech is the switch that leaves you with silent text.

Colourblind

Two independent rows. Colorblind Filter recolours the whole screen (Protanopia / Deuteranopia / Tritanopia). Colorblind-Safe Cues changes the gameplay cue colours — hostiles red→orange, friendlies green→cyan. Turn the second one on even if you leave the first off: it reaches NPC outlines, hover names, the enemy health bar, minimap dots and the hostile lock-on ring, so you're never reading the red/green axis.

09 Troubleshooting

Most of these are intended behaviour that reads as a bug. Check here before you write a report.

I press Space at a wall and nothing happens. G does nothing. I can't slide.

Correct behaviour on a fresh save. Those are implants you have to install. Check the Implants tab (I) — the chip must be both installed and active.

No enemies show on my map.

You need a scanner implant. Without one the body channel is completely blank at any zoom on both maps. Also check Accessibility → NPCs On Minimap.

My gun won't come out.

You're carrying a physics prop. Weapon draw is locked while your hands are full — set it down first.

The first click doesn't shoot.

Your weapon was holstered; that click drew it. Release and click again.

I got shot while in a menu.

Intended. Nothing but a conversation pauses the world.

The trainer won't sell me a stat point even though I have cash.

A paid stat raise is refused while your ledger is negative, and can never be funded on credit. Settle up at an ATM first.

My run stopped working.

You emptied the stamina bar. Sprint is locked out for three seconds. Jump comes back much sooner.

My reload wasted ammo.

By design. Ammo is counted in whole magazines, so reloading a partial mag discards it. Reload when empty.

The level restarted itself.

Did you hit End? It reloads the current level and is not gated out of this build. Avoid it.

I got shot through a fence.

Mesh, wire, glass and foliage stop bullets from neither side. Not a bug — find solid cover.

The screen went grey.

Either you're falling toward a lethal landing (full grey means the next impact kills you at your current HP), or you're low on health — that's a separate vignette that starts almost as soon as you're hurt.

My mouse feels sluggish when I'm moving fast.

Deliberate. Look sensitivity scales down above 6.5 m/s, reaching half at 12 m/s, so hop-runs stay controllable.

I changed a setting and nothing happened.

Did you press Apply? Everything except key rebinds and the music folder is staged until you do.

I loaded my autosave and the world was reset.

The autosave is a profile checkpoint. It doesn't restore looted containers, dead NPCs, dropped loot, spawned enemies or NPC positions, and all guns come back full. Only F5 and the three manual slots carry the exact-world layer.

All the item names say [PH].

Placeholder copy. Chips, weapon mods, quests and perks all currently ship with [PH]-prefixed names, and chip descriptions are empty. Not worth reporting.

Everything says the game is connected to the internet.

That's the first-launch warning card and the joke Terms screen. You can't skip them on a first launch; later launches let any key through.

10 About This Build

Some systems may not be reachable yet

This manual documents every system that exists in the game. In a playtest build, not all of them necessarily have a place in the level you're playing. If you can't find a trainer, a merchant, a healer or a quest-giver anywhere, that's the build you were given — not something you missed. Report what you couldn't find; don't spend an hour hunting for it.

Known placeholder copy

Implant chips, weapon mods, quest titles and perks all display a [PH] prefix, and chip descriptions are blank. The Controls tab has one placeholder hint row. These are known.

Worth reporting

  • Anything that reads as a crash, a freeze, or a hard softlock.
  • A setting that appears to save but has no effect after a restart — name the exact row.
  • The window disappearing after leaving fullscreen.
  • The left-handed weapon option looking wrong.
  • Anywhere the manual above disagrees with what the game actually did.

Everything in this manual was read from the game's source rather than observed in play, so treat specific numbers as the intended tuning rather than a measurement.
